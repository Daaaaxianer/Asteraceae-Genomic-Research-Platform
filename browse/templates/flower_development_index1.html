Flower development genes

  We selected Arabidopsis thaliana as the model organism and identified its Flower development genes. By best matching, we identified the Flower development genes of 25 other Fabaceae. They were identified using pfam and stored in the platform.
   Flower development is an important integrated developmental process in the life cycle of angiosperms, involving the transition from infinite to finite growth and different developmental modes, including flowering induction, signaling, attribute determination, and organogenesis, which is both induced by environmental factors and regulated by its own internal factors, through a series of signal transduction processes to initiate the control genes in the flowering decision process. Under the regulation of a complex network of gene interactions, the vegetative meristem (VM) transforms into inflorescence meristem (IM), and then flanks the IM to form floral meristem (FM), which differentiates into floral organs. Using Arabidopsis as a model species, our database counts 1,706 flowering genes from 25 legumes for easy browsing and downloading by researchers.
  The relevant gene ids can be obtained from the Fabaceae blast and match under the Tools module.This link is Fabaceae blast and match.
