Introduction: Chickpea (scientific name: Cicer arietinum, English: Chickpea), also known as horse bean, peach bean, chickpea, chickpea, etc., is native to southeastern Turkey, gradually domesticated and expanded to the Levant, and is now a major component of Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and Indian cuisine; in 2019, India accounted for 70% of global chickpea production. Chickpeas are also widely consumed in Europe, where they are often found in fiction and are commonly used as a medicinal herb in Uyghur medicine. It is named chickpea because of its peculiar shape, with a tip like a hawk's beak. The starch of chickpeas has a chestnut flavor. The white variety is similar to lotus seeds in taste and appearance. Chickpea flour with milk powder makes soy milk powder, which is easy to absorb and digest, and is a nutritious food for infants and the elderly. Chickpeas can also be made into various snacks and fried beans. The seeds can be used as diuretics, milk promoters, and can treat insomnia, prevent skin diseases and combat biliary diseases. Starch is widely used in the paper industry and textile industry.
