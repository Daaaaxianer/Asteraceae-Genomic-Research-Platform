Overview

Specie: Vigna umbellata

Taxonomy: Fabaceae; Papilionoideae; 50 kb inversion clade; NPAAA clade; indigoferoid/millettioid clade; Phaseoleae; Vigna

Abbr: Vum

Level: Chromosome

chromosome number: 2n=2X=22

Introduction: Herbs annual. Stems slender, to 1 m or more, yellow villous when young, glabrous when old. Leaves pinnately compound with 3 leaflets; stipules peltate, lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate, 10-15 mm long, acuminate at both ends; stipules subulate, leaflets papery, ovate or lanceolate, 10-13 cm long, (2-)5-7.5 cm wide, apex acute, base broadly cuneate or obtuse, entire or slightly 3-lobed, thinly sparsely hairy along veins on both surfaces, with 3 basal veins. Racemes axillary, short, with 2-3 flowers; bracts lanceolate; pedicels short, with glands at attachment; flowers yellow, ca. 1.8 cm long, ca. 1.2 cm wide; keeled petals with long angular appendages on right side. Pods linear-terete, pendulous, 6-10 cm long, ca. 5 mm wide, glabrous, 6-10 seeds, long ellipsoid, usually dark red, sometimes brown, black or straw yellow, 3-3.5 mm in diameter, seed hilum depressed. May-August. Wild or cultivated in southern China. Native to tropical Asia, also cultivated in Korea, Japan, Philippines, and other Southeast Asian countries. The seeds are used for food; they are used as medicine, and have the effect of promoting blood circulation, tonifying the blood, strengthening the spleen, removing dampness, and inducing diuresis to reduce swelling.

Distribution: Assam, Bangladesh, China South-Central, China Southeast, East Himalaya, Hainan, India, Japan, Jawa, Laccadive Is., Laos, Lesser Sunda Is., Malaya, Myanmar, New Guinea, Pakistan, Philippines, Solomon Is., Sri Lanka, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam, West Himalaya
